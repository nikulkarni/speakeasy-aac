SpeakEasy

A free app that lets people who can't use their hands or voice speak — just by moving their face in front of a camera. Everything runs on your device; no video is ever uploaded or saved.

Version 5 — command board · recommended

Speaks whole commands in one or two selections — smart-home control (“Alexa, turn on the AC”), urgent needs, and calls. Built for people for whom letter-by-letter typing is too slow. Choose the wake word for Alexa, Google, or Siri.

Version 4 — keyboard + phrases

Free-text typing with word + next-word prediction and a quick phrase board. The fallback for spelling anything the command board doesn't cover.

Version 3 — scanning mode

Head-pointer typing, word prediction, and scanning mode with a selectable switch (eyebrow raise, mouth open, blink, or the Space key) for users who can't control head or gaze.

Version 2 — with word prediction

The nose-tracking keyboard plus word suggestions that cut how many selections it takes to type.

Version 1 — nose-tracking keyboard

The original prototype: move your head to point, dwell or blink to select, and speak aloud.
To use: open a version, click “Turn on camera & start,” and allow camera access. Look straight ahead and press Recenter, then move your head to drive the cursor. Works best in Chrome, in decent lighting. On a phone or tablet, hold the device steady or prop it up.
Built for the Congressional App Challenge. Face tracking by MediaPipe Face Landmarker; speech by the browser’s Web Speech API. Open source — see the repository README.
